# Signal families

The four families of signal and what each must declare. Every signal belongs to exactly one family; the family sets the
minimum it owes the reader on top of the [six required elements](./required-elements.md).

---

## Warnings

A warning tells the user something may be wrong, stale, incomplete, or risky. It names the issue directly, without
hedging that obscures the finding, and it claims no certainty beyond what the underlying detection supports.

A warning **must** declare its reason for firing. A warning without a declared reason is not a warning; it is noise.

## Rankings and prioritisation

A ranking orders a set so the user can attend to the top of it. A sorted list with no declared question is numerology
with spacing.

A ranking surface **must** declare:

- what is being ranked;
- which criterion or question the ranking answers;
- which inputs and assumptions shape it;
- what the score or position represents.

A ranking that shows only the sorted result, without its governing criterion, **must not** be presented as a primary
signal. Rankings also **must not** stack-rank themselves against signals of other families without a declared
cross-family criterion.

## Recommendations

A recommendation states a suggested action and the path to challenge it. A recommendation that cannot be interrogated is
a product smell.

A recommendation surface **must** show:

- the recommendation, stated plainly;
- the reasoning path that produced it;
- the evidence or inputs it depends on;
- the review-and-challenge path — how to interrogate or override it.

Recommendations are not instructions. They are Generated or Inferred suggestions that **must** have explicit human
acceptance before any model mutation, per [the authority rule](./authority-rule.md).

## Review tasks

When a signal creates work, that work becomes explicit and enters the shared task infrastructure.

A review-task surface **must** show:

- what or who needs review;
- what triggered the task;
- what evidence is attached;
- the acceptance criterion — what "done" looks like.

Review tasks generated by [Metis](../../05-modules/metis/README.md), [Chrona](../../05-modules/chrona/README.md), or
[Continuum](../../05-modules/continuum/README.md) sit in the **same task infrastructure** as human-created work. Their
origin is always declared, and their completion **requires a human action** — a task never resolves itself. A review
task is in the **Awaiting review** result state (§9) until a person acts on it.

## Worked example

Metis ranks applications by dependency concentration and emits a **ranking** with criterion "inbound critical `realises`
and `hosts` relationships per application", placing `Insight Hub` (`n:application:insight-hub`) first. The user accepts
the top entry, which creates a **review task**: "Confirm whether `Insight Hub` concentration is acceptable given its Run
lifecycle." The task carries the contributing relationships (`e:insight-realises-insight`, criticality High) as
evidence, and its acceptance criterion is a recorded steward decision. The task stays Awaiting review until the steward
acts.
